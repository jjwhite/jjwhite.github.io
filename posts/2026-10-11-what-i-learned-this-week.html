Deep Dive

Guitar

The scale pattern I shared last week was just for major scales. Minor scales follow a similar pattern of:

W-H-W-W-H-W-W

Triads

A triad is a three note combination that makes up chords and are built off of the scales:

  • Major Chords: Root Note + Major 3rd + Perfect 5th (Frets: 0, 4, 7)
  • Minor Chords: Root Note + Minor 3rd + Perfect 5th (Frets: 0, 3, 7)
  • Sus2 Chords: Root Note + Major 2nd + Perfect 5th (Frets: 0, 2, 7)
  • Sus4 Chords: Root Note + Perfect 4th + Perfect 5th (Frets: 0, 5, 7)
  • Diminished Chords: Root Note + Minor 3rd + Lowered 5th (Frets: 0, 3, 6)
  • Augmented Chords: Root Note + Major 3rd + Raised 5th (Frets: 0, 4, 8)

Notice how the majority of these is just a change to the "middle" note. This is seen easily with the E and Em chords:

2 3 1
2 3

The only difference between the major and minor chord is that the 4th string is a G# in the major and an open G in the minor. So, that middle note in a major chord is a Major 3rd (G#) and in the E minor it's a Minor 3rd (G). In this particular case that is simply putting your finger down on the first fret of the G string for the major and leaving it open for the minor.

I'm still working on understanding harmonizing and the I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi, vii° notation. Not ready to write that in my own words yet.
